SenseLex Atlas

A tool for collecting and analysing multisensory lexical norms. It runs entirely in your browser, saves your data on this device and needs no installation and no login. Draw a controlled set of words from a lexical database, rate them and read the norms back.

How this works

The work runs across three tabs. Build & manage is where you assemble the word list: draw a frequency-controlled sample from the built-in banks, add your own words and manage languages and backups. Collect presents those words one at a time for sensorimotor rating or free naming. Explore turns the collected ratings into norms you can sort and download.

Everything stays in this browser on this device. There is no account, no upload and no server. Removing a word, or resetting, changes only your own copy here, so use Export to keep a copy or move your data to another machine.

Sensorimotor norms

Means across raters on each channel, with the dominant perceptual modality, the maximum perceptual strength, the modality-exclusivity score and a rater-spread figure as a reliability cue. Concreteness and Zipf frequency come from the word bank. Click a column heading to sort.

Codability from free naming

Agreement is the chance that two participants give a stimulus the same name. Shorter, more agreed responses mean higher codability.

Word and cue list

These are the items participants rate or name. Add your own item below, or paste a list where each line is language, conceptId, gloss, word, concreteness (the concreteness field is optional). Each row has a Remove button that takes that word out of your own list on this device only. Nothing is uploaded or shared, no other user is affected and any ratings you have already saved for it are kept in your data. Translations beyond English are a starting point and should be checked by a fluent speaker before live data collection.

Back up, restore and reset

Your data lives only in this browser. Export it to keep a copy, to move it to another machine or to load it into the full Atlas. Import merges a previously exported file without duplicating records.

The app reuses the datasets, the rating instrument and the measures listed below. Please cite them if you use the words or the norms.

  • Brysbaert, M., Warriner, A. B., & Kuperman, V. (2014). Concreteness ratings for 40 thousand generally known English word lemmas. Behavior Research Methods, 46(3), 904–911. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-013-0403-5
  • Lynott, D., & Connell, L. (2009). Modality exclusivity norms for 423 object properties. Behavior Research Methods, 41(2), 558–564. https://doi.org/10.3758/BRM.41.2.558
  • Lynott, D., Connell, L., Brysbaert, M., Brand, J., & Carney, J. (2020). The Lancaster Sensorimotor Norms: Multidimensional measures of perceptual and action strength for 40,000 English words. Behavior Research Methods, 52(3), 1271–1291. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-019-01316-z
  • Speer, R. (2022). rspeer/wordfreq: v3.0 (Version v3.0.2) [Computer software]. Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7199437
  • van Heuven, W. J. B., Mandera, P., Keuleers, E., & Brysbaert, M. (2014). SUBTLEX-UK: A new and improved word frequency database for British English. Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology, 67(6), 1176–1190. https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2013.850521

SenseLex is open-source research infrastructure. The word banks are a derived feature subset drawn from the sources cited above, curated to genuine content words rather than shipped as raw frequency lists. A few languages, including Arabic, Hebrew, Tamil, Vietnamese, Turkish and Japanese, currently use a small hand-checked core list instead of a full bank, because a repeated curation check found the derived bank still too noisy to ship; see the project repository for the audit figures. This single page is the lightweight, browser-only edition. The full Atlas adds shared online collection, an offline-first field client and a server database.
